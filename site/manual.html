Prysm manual

Prysm is a 4-voice synthesizer firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1. Each voice has two oscillators, and each oscillator is a little modulation chain of three wavetable operators, and a noise source (six kinds) beside them; each of the three goes through a resonant filter (12 or 24 dB low-pass, or a comb) or straight past it. Then a high-pass, an amp envelope, an LFO, two looping mod envelopes, a mod matrix, a phaser / flanger, a bit-crusher and stereo chorus, delay and reverb. Alongside it a drum machine (kick, snare, hat, clap; three kits) plays a lane of its own in every sequencer pattern, the sequencer's steps can lock knobs, and a looper records what you play over it.

A sound can also be two synths in one: part A on osc 1 and part B on osc 2, each with its own filter, envelopes and LFO, played together, side by side on the keys or one at a time (Parts). Each part can load a tone from a library of 96 (and 64 of your own: Tones); each oscillator can have a sampled attack transient (TRANS); and RANDOM rolls a new sound, or nudges the one you have (Patches).

Installing

From the web installer (Chrome or Edge, on a computer): open the Prysm installer page, connect the FM-1 by USB straight to the computer, press Install Prysm, allow MIDI access when the browser asks, and wait for the FM-1 to restart. Do not unplug it while it writes.

From the command line (a built package, see the README):

python3 tools/fm1_install.py prysm-1.6.fwsc

Coming from another firmware. Most community firmwares (Felucca, SLOOP, Dinghy and their family, and the official M-VAVE firmware) accept Prysm directly. ETCH only accepts its own updates and the official firmware: restore the official V15 first (ETCH's own installer has RESTORE - OFFICIAL V15), then install Prysm.

Going back. The FM-1 Firmware Hub installs the official firmware and every community firmware; Prysm answers its updater like the others.

Newer FM-1s. Units sold from autumn 2026 (mostly on AliExpress) have a different flash chip, a UC25HQ80. Prysm supports it from 1.4.1 on; older Prysm releases refuse to install on these units (the FM-1 keeps the firmware it has). If the USB rescue screen (OCT- held at power-on) shows UNKNOWN FLASH and a code, please report the code.

Prysm is a community firmware, not made or supported by M-VAVE. Installing it is at your own risk.

Playing

When the FM-1 starts, rainbow static collapses into the word PRYSM (press any key or button to skip it), then the OSC 1 page. The sound is the last patch you saved or loaded.
Keys play; 4 voices. The key LEDs show what sounds. The notes also go out over USB-MIDI.
OCT- / OCT+ the keyboard's octave (-2 .. +2; remembered when the FM-1 is switched off)
MASTER the volume
MIDI in over USB or the FM-1's TRS MIDI IN jack, alike: notes play Prysm with their velocity (see AMP's VEL row), on any channel or the one set on SETUP; the MIDI clock and START / STOP from either

When a fifth note comes, the voice that is quietest (or, if all are held, the oldest) gives way.

Finding your way around

Every page works the same way:

 OSC 1 > MOD1                     3* [#]  <- page > row          slot, unsaved (*), battery
 ----------------------------------------
  CAR       MOD1      MOD2
 [carrier] <- [ mod 1 ] <- [ mod 2 ]       <- the page's picture: what the row touches is white
 ----------------------------------------
  BANK     WAVE     RATIO    AMT           <- the selected row: its four knobs' names,
  BASIC    SINE     x2       70            <- their values, large,
                             ----          <- and a bar under each level
 ----------------------------------------

Turning a knob lights its own cell for a moment: framed, its name bright, its value drifting blue as you turn it up and red as you turn it down, fading back to white. The picture stays in view while you change it. PRESETS (the cutoff) lights the CUT cell where one shows it (FILTER, LIVE); on other pages it shows large in the picture's top right corner, as do messages and confirmations.

Envelope times show in ms and s, as the engine plays them (1 ms .. 10 s). A control that does nothing as the sound is now stays where it is but is dimmed, with the reason in the row's empty cells: COMB's DAMP and SPREAD on a low-pass (COMB ONLY), HP RES with the high-pass off, LFO 2's RATE while SYNC is on (SYNCED), MOD FX's knobs while its TYPE is OFF.

The header's battery shows 0 to 3 bars (red, green, blue: a prism); empty, it blinks: charge the FM-1 soon. Connected to a computer it shows a bolt (USB power) instead, as the FM-1 cannot tell its charge while it is charging. The splash shows it too, and the SETUP page in words, with the raw reading (ADC).

CPU: how much of the FM-1's time the sound takes (its peak over the last half second) is on the SETUP page, a figure and a bar, bright past 70 %. Keep it under that: near 100 % the sound starts to crackle. The header says CPU (bright, before the slot) only while it is past 80 %.

Values go from 0 to 127 unless the page says otherwise. Bipolar values (shown with a sign, like +24) are 0 in the middle of their knob.

The pages

OSC 1 / OSC 2

Each oscillator is a chain: mod 2 -> mod 1 -> carrier. The carrier is what you hear; mod 1 changes the carrier, and mod 2 changes mod 1. The picture draws the three as boxes with the waves they are playing, and the arrows between them: an arrow gets thicker with more modulation, and the symbol on it is its mode (~ FM, x ring, + mix).

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4
CAR carrier bank carrier wave carrier ratio the oscillator's level
MOD1 mod 1 bank mod 1 wave mod 1 ratio (PIT1 in MIX / RING) mod 1 amount
ENV1 mod 1 attack mod 1 release mod 1's mode: FM, RING, MIX mod 1 fine
MOD2 mod 2 bank mod 2 wave mod 2 ratio (PIT2 in MIX / RING) mod 2 amount
ENV2 mod 2 attack mod 2 release mod 2's mode mod 2 fine
RATIO carrier ratio mod 1 ratio (PIT1) mod 2 ratio (PIT2) fine tune (the whole oscillator)

A row for each operator, then a row for each modulator's envelope and mode: everything about one operator is together, and the picture lights the operator the row works on. The RATIO row shows the three ratios side by side, to compare them (the same knobs as on the operators' rows).

Waves. Six banks of eight waves, from simple to complex. The wave knob moves smoothly through the bank, morphing from one wave into the next (the name shown is the nearest).

Bank Its waves
BASIC sine, rounded, triangle, soft saw, saw, square, pulse 30 %, pulse 12 %
HARM organ-like stacks of 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34 harmonics
PULSE pulse widths from square (50 %) to a thin 3 %
VOCAL vowels: oo, oh, ah, ae, eh, er, ih, ee
DIGI stepped and bit-reduced shapes
CMPLX folded sines, hard-synced saws, harmonic clusters

Ratio. Each of the three runs at a ratio to the note: 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3 ... 16. With FM, whole-number ratios give harmonic, instrument-like tones; 1.5 and 0.5 give hollow and sub tones. Fine detunes the whole oscillator by up to +-50 cents.

Modes. How a modulator acts on the wave it points at:

  • FM (phase modulation): bright, metallic, bell-like, the DX7 sound. More amount, more harmonics.
  • RING: multiplies; at full amount you hear the sum and difference of the two pitches, not the carrier itself.
  • MIX: adds the modulator's wave to the other one, a layer.

Pitch in MIX and RING. A modulator in MIX or RING is heard, not just felt, so a ratio is the wrong tool: its RATIO knob (on its row and on the RATIO row) becomes PIT1 (or PIT2), the modulator's pitch against the carrier, -24 .. +24 semitones. Switch back to FM and the ratio is there again, as you left it (each mode keeps its own setting). Each modulator's FINE (ENV1, ENV2 rows) detunes it by up to +-50 cents, in any mode: in MIX a chorused layer, in FM a slowly beating, inharmonic shimmer. The pitch is a modulation destination too (M1PITCH, M2PITCH, below).

Envelopes. Each modulator has its own attack and release (1 ms .. 10 s): the amount rises over the attack, holds while the key is down, and falls over the release. A slow mod 1 attack on FM makes a note that brightens as you hold it.

Amounts and level. Each modulator's AMT (on its row: how much it changes the next one) and the oscillator's own LEVEL (CAR row).

TRANS

An attack transient for each oscillator: a short sampled sound at the start of every note, laid over the oscillator, as the Roland D-50 put a sampled attack in front of a synthesised body. A hammer or a pluck in front of an FM tone, a breath before a pad, a click on a bass. EDIT three times (OSC 1, OSC 2, TRANS). Two rows, one for each oscillator; the picture shows both transients as they play at C4: the sound across, LEVEL its height, DECAY's fade over it, its length in milliseconds.

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4
OSC 1 TYPE (OFF: none) LEVEL TUNE (-24 .. +24 st) DECAY (FULL: as made)
OSC 2 TYPE LEVEL TUNE DECAY
  • A transient has its own envelope: the sound's own shape, faded sooner by DECAY (from FULL down). It does not follow the amp envelope, so it is heard at once even on a pad with a slow attack, and it goes past the filter (as the D-50's sampled attacks did). CRUSH, MOD FX and the effects still take it. A softer note's is quieter (AMP velocity, as the amp's).
  • It plays once at the start of each note (a slide in the sequencer: no new one). The split's other side and a LIVE mute of its oscillator silence it.
  • The pitched ones follow the key, made at C4: HAMMER, PLUCK, PICK, MALLET, KALIMB, BELL, GLASS, METAL, CHIFF, FLUTE, BOW, BRASS, SLAP, BLIP, VOX. The others play at their own pitch on every key: ANVIL, BREATH, KNOCK, CLICK, TICK, THUMP, ZAP, NOISE, SPIT. TUNE moves either.
  • All 24 are made from scratch by tools/gen_transients.py (physical and spectral models; no recordings), stored as 8-bit mu-law at 16 .. 32 kHz.

NOISE

A noise source in every voice, beside the two oscillators: it follows the amp envelope like them, and goes through the filter or past it (ROUTE). EDIT four times. The picture is the noise itself: its wave (a few milliseconds) above, its spectrum below (24 bands, 60 Hz .. 15 kHz, the loudest at the top), so you see its colour change as you turn.

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4
NOISE TYPE COLOUR (or RATE, DENSE) LEVEL (0: off) ROUTE: FILTER / DIRECT
  • WHITE: flat hiss. PINK: softer (-3 dB an octave), wind and surf. BROWN: darker still (-6 dB an octave), a rumble. For these three, COLOUR is a tone (127 open, down: darker).
  • DIGI: a game console's noise channel, a short-loop shift register: buzzy, metallic and pitched to the key (play it like an oscillator). RATE moves it +-48 semitones from the note.
  • S&H: random values held for a step, the step 16 times the note's: a crunchy, bit-crushed hiss that follows the keys. RATE as DIGI's.
  • CRACKLE: sparse clicks like an old record. DENSE: how many, about 2 .. 2000 a second.

The noise's level is on the MIX page too (N.LVL), beside the oscillators'.

FILTER

A filter for each voice, with drive before it, a high-pass after it, and its own envelope (on the F.ENV page: ENV, twice). TYPE picks what it is: a LP 12 (2-pole, 12 dB / octave) or LP 24 (4-pole, 24 dB / octave) low-pass, or a comb, COMB+ or COMB-. The picture draws the filter's actual response (the low-pass's curve or the comb's peaks, and the high-pass) and the envelope's shape.

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4
FILTER cutoff (16 Hz .. 20 kHz) resonance envelope amount (bipolar, +-60 semitones) keytrack (0 .. 100 %)
TYPE LP 12, LP 24, COMB+, COMB- drive (0 = clean) high-pass cutoff (OFF, 16 Hz .. 4.2 kHz) HP RES: the high-pass's resonance (0 = none; up: a peak at its cutoff, to +16 dB, soft-clipped when loud)
COMB damping (0 bright .. 127 dark) spread

The COMB row works on COMB+ and COMB- only: on a low-pass its knobs are dimmed (COMB ONLY). HP RES is dimmed while the high-pass is OFF.

Cutoff and resonance glide as you turn them, so sweeps are smooth. Keytrack at 100 % moves the cutoff with the note, so every key keeps the same brightness. A negative envelope amount closes the filter as the envelope rises.

LP 24 is steeper and darker than LP 12; its resonance is a little softer.

Self-oscillation: from RES 122 up, the low-pass sings on its own: a sine at the cutoff, held at a steady level from the lowest cutoffs to the highest, even with the oscillators' levels at 0. With KEY at 127 it follows the keys in tune (an octave up the keyboard, an octave up in pitch); CUT sets where: at about 47 each key sings near its own note (a little sharp: tune by ear), each step of CUT about a semitone higher. The amp envelope still shapes it. Turn RES back below 122 and it rings and dies away as before.

COMB+ / COMB- turn the filter into a comb, as on a Waldorf: a row of resonant peaks at a frequency and every multiple of it. The FILTER row then plays the comb: CUT sets the first peak (in 3/4-semitone steps: 64 is C4, 80 is C5), RES its feedback (from no effect to long ringing), ENV sweeps it with the envelope and KEY tracks the keyboard. With KEY at 127 (100 %) and CUT at 64, the peaks sit on each note's own harmonics: a plucked string from a short burst, with a long amp release. COMB+ rings on the peaks, metallic and plucked; COMB- puts them halfway between, hollow and an octave down. On the COMB row, DAMP darkens the ringing and SPREAD stretches the upper peaks further and further apart while the first stays put: from an even comb to bells, bars and metal. The peaks get louder as RES rises (up to about +17 dB, rounded off rather than clipped), so the comb keeps its level even when they miss the harmonics. High feedback takes a moment to build up on low notes.

The LFO and the mod envelopes move the comb through CUTOFF, and its spread through SPREAD.

AMP

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4
ENV attack decay sustain release
VEL velocity to level velocity to cutoff velocity to modulation
VCA OPEN: the amp held open this much (0 = closed)

Times go from 1 ms to 10 s.

OPEN (VCA row) holds the amp open whatever the envelope does, as a VCA bias on an analog synth (the Neutron's, say): an instant drone. Turn it up and the oscillators sound at once, at the pitch they were last played at (C3 when nothing has been played since power-on), the whole sound: FM, filter and all. While a voice drones, its other envelopes (the modulators', the filter's, the mod envelopes) hold as if its key were down, so every knob works on it live: turn MOD1's AMT and the drone brightens. Keys play over it with their envelope (from the drone's level up), and the notes you let go drone on, so a chord let go becomes a drone chord. The picture draws the level as a line across the envelope. Turn OPEN back to 0 and the drone releases and fades out. (A drone keeps up to four voices running: watch the CPU on SETUP with every effect on.)

Velocity comes from MIDI: a keyboard or a DAW playing Prysm over USB. The FM-1's own keys have no velocity (they are on / off), so they always play at full velocity and these settings do not change them. Each sensitivity says how much a softer note is quieter (by the square of its velocity, as it sounds natural), darker (up to 36 semitones of cutoff) or less modulated (less FM brightness: the classic expressive FM touch). At 0, velocity changes nothing.

M.ENV 1 / M.ENV 2

Two mod envelopes, each an ADSR started by every note, with a destination and an amount of its own (ENV three and four times).

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4
ENV attack decay sustain release
DEST destination amount (bipolar) M.ENV 2: PART (BOTH / A / B) LOOP ON / OFF

DEST and AMT are on KNOB1 and KNOB2, where the LFOs have them.

With LOOP ON an envelope runs attack -> decay -> attack -> decay ... for as long as you hold the key, then releases: a sustain of 0 gives full up-and-down sweeps, a higher sustain a shallower cycle. Its destination can be any of the LFO's (below), plus two of its own:
LFORATE the LFO's rate, up to +-4 octaves: an envelope that speeds the LFO up and slows it down
LFOAMT the LFO's depth, from nothing up to double
LFOSHAPE the LFO's wave, morphed: from the one you chose along SINE, TRI, SAW UP, SAW DN, SQUARE, S&H (a full amount: the whole row), each fading into the next

The two can share a destination (they add up), or split the work: say M.ENV 1 sweeps the cutoff while M.ENV 2, short and looping, flicks mod 1's pitch. The picture shows two seconds of the envelope (the key held for 1.5 s, the dotted line where it is let go) with its destination's name, and with LFORATE or LFOAMT the LFO under it, moving as the envelope moves it.

MATRIX

Press LFO a third time for the mod matrix: four more routes, each a source onto any of the LFO's destinations (the same list as the LFO's DEST), with an amount either way. They add to what the LFO, LFO 2 and the mod envelopes already do. A route is off while its source is OFF or its amount is 0 (the middle of the knob).

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4
MOD 1 .. MOD 4 SRC: the source DEST: the destination AMT: -64 .. +63 TIME: a drum source's fall (1 ms .. 10 s; others: --)
PARTS MOD 1's part: BOTH / A / B MOD 2's MOD 3's MOD 4's

LFO, LFO 2 the LFOs, as they run (a second route from the same LFO, at its own amount)
M.ENV 1, M.ENV 2 the mod envelopes

The picture lists the four routes, source to destination, each amount a bar from the middle. Some ideas: VELO onto the CUTOFF (a harder note, brighter); KEY onto MOD1 (high notes less FM, so they stay clean); WHEEL onto the LFO's pitch depth for vibrato on demand; RANDOM onto the pitch, a little, for an old synth's drift.

The drums as sources. A drum source follows that drum's hits, from the drum sequencer and from MIDI (channel 10, or the DRUM page's keys). It works even when the drum's LEVEL is 0, so a silent kick can still drive a route. A drum muted on the LIVE screen doesn't trigger it. On AMP, a negative amount ducks: the sound dips on each hit and comes back over TIME (sidechain pumping). A positive amount gates: the sound opens on each hit and closes over TIME, and a long TIME gives swells. The duck and gate act on the voices before the effects, so the delay and reverb tails carry on through them. Elsewhere the drum works like any envelope: KICK onto the CUTOFF opens the filter on every kick, and HAT onto MOD1 adds FM bite in time with the hats.

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4

Routing. Each source goes through the filter (its drive, the low-pass or the comb, the high-pass), or DIRECT: past it, straight to the amp (its envelope, VCA OPEN, the effects all still apply). Filter one oscillator and leave the other bright: a resonant sweep over a steady body, or a comb ringing on the noise while the oscillators stay clean. The picture shows each source's level and its route (DIR inverted).

Sends (DUAL). In DUAL each part already has its own filter, so the ROUTE row sends the parts to the effects instead: how much of each part goes into the delay and the reverb (127, the most, is how the other modes play: everything in). The effects' own MIX is how much comes back. A dry bass on part A and a pad drowned in reverb on part B: A REV 0, B REV 127. A send stays dimmed while its effect's MIX is 0 (DELAY OFF, REVERB OFF). The chorus, MOD FX and CRUSH stay on the whole sound. The oscillators' routes are kept, not shown: set them in another mode (the noise's is on the NOISE page). The picture shows each part's sends beside its level.

Glide (portamento) slides each new note from the pitch of the last one. ALWAYS slides every note; LEGATO only when another note is still held (play overlapping to slide, apart to jump). Arpeggiated notes glide too: with a tied gate and LEGATO, an acid line.

Split the keyboard: below the split key only OSC 1 plays, from it upward only OSC 2, so the two oscillators become two sounds side by side (a bass under a lead). Set the key with KNOB1, or turn LEARN and press the key you want (it is not played; a MIDI keyboard's note works too). OCT1 and OCT2 move each side up or down by octaves (a bass played in the middle of the keys, sounding low). The four voices are shared: any mix of the two sides, four notes at a time. The filter, the envelopes, the LFOs and the effects are the same for both; the noise plays on both sides. The sequencer, the arpeggiator and MIDI follow the split too, by each note's pitch. It is saved with the patch, the key and both octaves, so each patch has its own split (or none) and loading a patch brings its split with it; a change waits for SAVE like any other. The picture shows it under the levels (OSC1 < C4 < OSC2).

Parts. A sound can be two synths in one. Part A is OSC 1 and part B is OSC 2, and each part has its own filter (and high-pass), amp and filter envelopes, LFO, M.ENV 1, velocity, glide and noise. The effects, LFO 2, M.ENV 2 and the MATRIX are shared. MODE says how the parts play:

MODE Plays
SINGLE one synth, as before the parts: every note on both oscillators, through part A's filter and envelopes (part B's own settings unused). Every older patch loads as SINGLE and sounds as it did
DUAL the part you are editing: the keys (and the arp) play it; the sequencer and the looper play each note on its own part. The four voices are shared, so a part B note keeps sounding when you switch to A and play over it
SPLIT by the key: below the split key part A, from it part B (the SPLIT row's key and octaves)
LAYER every note on both parts at once, each through its own filter and envelopes: two notes at a time

Levels. With two parts, each has its own level and its own place in the stereo picture (the LEVELS row), set after its amp: A LVL and B LVL balance the two without changing how hard each drives its filter, and A PAN and B PAN spread them apart (a bass in the middle, a pad to the left, a lead to the right). The pan is a balance: at C a part sounds as it did, turned to one side the other side fades out. The effects come after, over both. In SINGLE they are not used.

On the PARTS and LEVELS rows the picture shows the two parts side by side: each part's wave, its filter, its level and its place, and its voices (the four squares, lit while one sounds on that part). The part you are editing is framed in its colour. In SINGLE part B is greyed: part A plays both oscillators.

With two parts, the SELECT button switches the part you are editing: it opens FILTER from any page, and on FILTER each press switches between part A and part B. The part stays chosen as you move to other pages. The header says what a page edits: A in amber or B in teal, with a thin rule of that colour under it, on the pages a part has its own settings on (OSC 1 is part A's oscillator, OSC 2 part B's); ALL in white on the shared pages (the effects, LFO 2, M.ENV 2, the MATRIX, ...). Every part page then turns that part's own settings (the cutoff on PRESETS too). RANDOM rolls the part you are on. On the pages a part has its own settings on, what you are editing is highlighted in the part's colour (muted amber or teal); text, and the shared pages, stay white.

The shared modulators reach both parts, or one: LFO 2's and M.ENV 2's PART (on their DEST rows) and each matrix route's (the MATRIX page's PARTS row) is BOTH, A or B. A whole-band tremolo on part B alone, a filter sweep on A while B holds still. A modulator on one part shows that part's letter in its picture. LFO 2 on the effects (CHORUS, DELAY, REVERB, DLYTIME, DLYFDBK, REVSIZE) moves them for both, as the effects are shared. In SINGLE every modulator reaches the one synth, whatever its PART says. In DUAL, LAYER and SPLIT each part takes its octave from the SPLIT row's OCT1 (part A) and OCT2 (part B). Part B's MIDI channel is on the SETUP page.

ARP

The arpeggiator plays the notes you hold, one at a time, in time. ARP (pressed again on the ARP page) or the ARP cell turns it on or off (its LED lights). While it is on, the keys and MIDI notes go to it instead of straight to the voices.

Row KNOB1 KNOB2 KNOB3 KNOB4

  • Gate is how long each note sounds in its step; at TIE they join with no gap (with glide LEGATO: slides).
  • Swing lengthens every other step and shortens the next: 50 % is straight, 67 % a triplet feel, 75 % a dotted one.
  • LATCH: the notes keep playing after you let go; the next chord (after all the keys are up) replaces them.
  • Its notes start and stop to within a third of a millisecond, whatever the screen is doing.
  • The picture shows the next 16 steps, as long and as far apart as they will be, the one playing in white.

On the MIDI clock (SETUP: CLOCK = MIDI) the arpeggiator follows a DAW or another instrument instead of its own tempo: 24 clock ticks a beat, a step every 24, 12, 8, 6, 4 or 3 ticks for 1/4 .. 1/32. The BPM cell then shows the clock's tempo (EXT while none comes); swing and gate still apply. With CLOCK = MIDI and no clock running, the arpeggiator waits.

FX

Effects after the voices: crush -> mod FX -> chorus -> delay -> reverb, a page each: FX steps through CHORUS, DELAY, REVERB, MOD FX and CRUSH. Each one is off while its MIX (CRUSH: its BITS and RATE) is at 0. The chorus, delay and reverb pages draw the chain, with a mix bar under each effect and the page's own lit. CRUSH and MOD FX work on the synth's voices only, before the chorus: the drums have a crush of their own (DRUM page, BUS row).

  • Chorus: left and right swept against each other, for width. Full mix is half wet: the classic chorus.
  • Delay: ping sends the repeats from side to side. Tone darkens each repeat. Turning the time while it plays bends the repeats, like tape.
  • Sync: with SYNC on, TIME picks a note length of the tempo instead of ms: 1/32, 1/16T, 1/16, 1/8T, 1/16D, 1/8, 1/4T, 1/8D, 1/4, 1/2T, 1/4D, 1/2. The tempo is the arp's BPM, or the MIDI clock when CLOCK is MIDI, and the delay follows it as it changes. A length longer than the delay's 735 ms (slow tempos) is halved until it fits, so it stays in time. LFO 2 on DLYTIME steps through the lengths.
  • Wow: sweeps the delay time, so the repeats wobble in pitch.
  • Shift: each repeat is pitch-shifted: +12 makes the echoes climb an octave at a time, -5 makes them fall in fourths. (The first repeat is the note as played.)
  • Reverb: a plate. Size sets how long the tail lasts, damping how quickly its highs fade, pre-delay the gap before it starts.
  • R.MOD: sweeps the reverb's inside, so the tail shimmers and wobbles around the note.
  • Shimmer: the tail climbs in octaves as it fades. It never runs away, at any setting.
  • Phaser: six stages, their notches swept up and down by the rate and depth, around MANUAL (low: a deep, slow whoosh; high: a thin one). Feedback deepens the notches; turned down (negative), it hollows the sound instead.
  • Flanger: a short delay (0.2 .. 8 ms, MANUAL) swept against the dry sound: a jet. With feedback it rings at the delay's pitch; negative feedback gives the hollower, metallic flange. MIX at about half is the classic one (full mix is all delay, a vibrato).
  • Crush: BITS takes the sound down to fewer bits (grit at 12, broken at 4), RATE holds each sample longer (a lower sample rate, with the aliasing of an old sampler). The picture is a sine as it comes out.

With every effect on, Prysm uses roughly twice the CPU: watch it on the SETUP page.

SEQ

A polyphonic step sequencer, 303-style: notes and chords of up to four notes, rests, accents, slides and ties, in 4 patterns of up to 64 steps, and a song that plays the patterns in an order you choose, each so many times. Enter steps one at a time, or record in real time as it plays. It plays alongside the keys (and the arpeggiator), at the ARP page's tempo and swing, on its own clock or the MIDI clock. Each patch slot has a sequencer of its own (the patterns, the song, the drum kit): LOAD brings it in with the sound. To try other sounds over the same song, load them with SOUND instead (see Patches).

The picture is the pattern being edited: 64 squares, 16 a row. An empty square is a rest, a grey one a note, a white one an accent; a little bar joins a sliding step to the next; a tie is a bar carrying the note before it on; past the length, dots. A chord's other notes are notches across the top of its square (a short notch: one of them tied on). Lines mark the beats, four to a row (a row is a bar of 16 steps). The cursor, the step you edit, is ringed (doubled while recording); a bar under a square shows the step playing. The header says which pattern you are editing (SEQ P1 .. P4), or SEQ SONG in song mode. On the SONG and ENTRY rows the picture is the song instead: its entries, each a pattern and how many times it plays.

Entering notes (stopped): press REC (its LED lights, the header says SEQ REC), move to a step with KNOB1 (or start at the first) and play a key: its note goes into the step (a rest or a tie becomes a note), and when you let go the cursor moves to the next, so you can type a line in. Hold keys together for a chord: they all go into the step, and the cursor moves on once you let them all go. MIDI notes enter the same way. Press REC again to play the keys normally.

Recording in real time (playing): press PLAY, then REC, and play. Or press REC first, then PLAY: you get a count-in, a bar of ticks (whatever CLICK is set to), and recording starts on the downbeat; a note played just before it lands on step 1. (On the MIDI clock there is no count-in: the clock's START is the downbeat.) Each note lands on the nearest step (the one playing, or the next if you are past its middle), with its velocity (the FM-1's keys: full; a MIDI keyboard: as played), and a held note is tied over the steps you held it through. Recording overdubs: what is there stays, and each pass adds to it, up to four notes a step (playing a note that is already in the step records it again, at the new velocity). While you hold a key, the pattern's note in that place rests, so you hear yourself, not both. You can record from any page (turn the filter as you go; on the DRUM page the drum keys record hits, see DRUM); REC or PLAY ends it. To take something out, use the STEP and CHORD rows, or CLEAR a row.

  • PLAY starts from the first step (of the pattern, or of the song) and stops.
  • The metronome (CLICK row) ticks every beat while the sequencer plays, higher on each bar's first beat (4/4, counted from PLAY), whatever the step size. REC ticks only while you record. It is dry (no effects on it), goes out over USB audio with the rest, and stays as you set it when you load another sound (it is a setting, as the MIDI ones are).
  • ACCENT plays the step at full velocity, the others a little softer: how much louder, brighter and more modulated an accent is comes from the AMP page's VEL row.
  • SLIDE holds the step's note into the next one and glides to its pitch in about 30 ms, without a new attack (the 303 slide). A slide into a rest just ends. In a chord, each note slides to the next step's note in its place.
  • TIE carries the note before it on, at its pitch, without a new attack: a note followed by two ties is one note three steps long (its gate ends in the last tie). A tie after a rest is a rest. A chord's notes tie each on their own: a recorded chord whose notes were let go one by one keeps them as you played them.
  • Chords play as up to four voices: the same four the keys use, so a four-note chord over a held chord steals the oldest notes.
  • Patterns: PATT on the PATTERN row picks the one you edit; in PATT mode it is also the one playing. Changing it while the sequencer plays switches at the end of the current pattern, so it stays in time. Each pattern has its own length; the step and the gate are the same for all.
  • Copy and paste (EDIT row): pick a row of 16 steps with KNOB1, turn COPY, pick another row (or another pattern on the PATTERN row) and turn PASTE. With the row on ALL, COPY and PASTE take the whole pattern, its length included; a single row pasted into ALL fills all four rows. CLEAR empties the row (with ALL, the whole pattern).
  • Asking first. PASTE, CLEAR, CLR1 / CLR2, INIT, the DRUM page's CLEAR, PASTE and LOAD, and the LOOP page's CLEAR change nothing at once: turned, they say exactly what they will change (say CLEAR P1 ROW 2) and wait. Press SAVE to confirm, HOME to cancel (another button, row or page, or 6 seconds, cancel too). COPY just copies, and says what.
  • Two parts (PARTS row), with the split on: the notes below the split key are part 1 (they play OSC 1), from it upward part 2 (OSC 2). Record a bass line on the low keys, then on the next passes a lead on the high keys: each pass overdubs, and while you hold a key only its own part's notes rest, so the other part keeps playing under you. MUTE1 and MUTE2 silence a part (saved with the sequencer); CLR1 and CLR2 clear a part's notes in the EDIT row's row (or ALL), leaving the other part and the drums. A step holds up to four notes across both parts. Which part a note is in follows the split: move the split key and notes change sides. With the split off, every note is part 1's.
  • DUAL (MIX's PARTS row): each note carries its own part, A or B, so one pattern plays both synths. A note recorded or entered goes on the part you are editing (SELECT on FILTER), or, from a MIDI keyboard, on its channel's part (the SETUP page's B IN). Record a bass on part A, switch to part B (SELECT, SELECT), overdub a lead on it. The PART row shows and changes the parts of the step under the cursor; steps with a note on part B have a small teal mark in their corner. Here MUTE1 / MUTE2 and CLR1 / CLR2 are parts A and B. In SINGLE, SPLIT and LAYER a note's part is kept but not used. The arpeggiator plays the part you are editing; the looper keeps each note on the part it was played on.
  • The song (SONG and ENTRY rows): set MODE to SONG, its LENGTH to the number of entries, then for each entry its pattern and how many times it plays. PLAY then plays the entries in turn and starts again after the last.
  • INIT (SONG row) starts over: all four patterns empty, the song P1, P2, P3, P4 once each, PATT mode. It asks first: SAVE to confirm.
  • Saving: the patterns and the song save themselves into the slot whose sequencer it is, a few seconds after a change, once nothing is playing (the flash write would cut a note short). They come back at power-on. From Prysm 1.2 and older (one sequencer for all), every used slot starts with a copy of it; from 0.8 and older, the pattern of the sound that comes back at power-on becomes pattern 1.
  • On the MIDI clock (SETUP: CLOCK = MIDI): START plays from the first step, STOP stops, as for the arpeggiator; PLAY must be on.
  • Parameter locks (LOCK row): each pattern has two lock lanes, each one a parameter: CUTOFF, RES, F.ENV (the filter envelope's amount), DRIVE, A.DEC and A.REL (the amp envelope's decay and release), F.DEC (the filter envelope's decay), FM1 and FM2 (the oscillators' modulation), DLY SND or REV SND. Pick the step with the STEP row's cursor (KNOB1), then on the LOCK row set its value with VAL1 or VAL2: the notes that start on that step play with the knob there instead of where the sound has it, and the next step without a lock goes back. A locked step has a mark in its corner. A lock is part of the step (the EDIT row's COPY, PASTE and CLEAR take it with the notes; a lock pasted into a pattern whose lane is OFF brings the lane's parameter), the lane's parameter part of the pattern. DLY SND and REV SND set how much of the synth goes into the delay and reverb for that step alone: a single echo thrown on the last note of a phrase, a snare-like reverb burst on one chord.

DRUM

A drum machine of four drums, KICK, SNARE, HAT (closed; an accent opens it) and CLAP, each a lane of steps in every pattern, under the notes: they play together, from the same steps, in the song too. SEQ twice. The sounds were made from scratch (no recordings) in the style of the classic drum machines, in three kits (KIT row):

Kit
LO-FI stored as an old sampler would keep them, 8 bits at a low rate (11 kHz the kick, 16 kHz the snare and clap, 22 kHz the hats), played back without smoothing: crunchy by design (Prysm 1.3's kit, with a clap)
TR clean and bright, 16 bits: a punchy kick, a crisp snare, metallic hats
BOOM 16 bits, deep: a long, low kick, a fat snare, a wide clap

On the DRUM page the keys are the drum machine's, as on a TR (they play no notes here):

  • The 16 white keys (F3 .. G5) are the 16 steps on screen. Press one: a hit; again: an accent; (the snare) again: a flam; again: none. Their LEDs show the lane (an accent blinks, a flam faster; the step playing flips its LED as it passes).

  • Recording drums in real time: on the DRUM page, press REC (the header says DRUM P1 REC; the page stays) and PLAY (with the count-in if REC came first), then tap F#3, G#3, A#3, C#4 in time.   • MIDI: notes on channel 10 play the drums (General MIDI's: 36 kick, 38 or 40 snare, 42 or 44 closed hat, 46 open hat, 39 clap), whatever the IN channel, with their velocity. So a DAW's drum track plays them.

While the sequencer plays, the new one takes over where the next pattern would start (the sound changes at once), from the top of its pattern or song; stopped, at once. A slot whose sound came in from the editor alone has no sequencer yet: LOAD keeps the one playing, and it becomes that slot's. LOAD does not save the sequencer it replaces: pattern changes it has not saved yet (they save a few seconds after a change, once nothing plays) are let go.
  • SOUND brings the slot's sound back alone: the sequencer plays on, still saving into the slot it came from.
  • INIT loads the default sound (the sequencer plays on). If you have unsaved changes, LOAD, SOUND and INIT ask first, saying what would happen (DISCARD EDITS? SAVE: LOAD 4): press SAVE again, or HOME to keep them.
  • UNDO takes the last change back: the sound and the sequencer as they were before it. A change is a knob turned after a pause (2 seconds: a sweep of turns is one change), or an action: a PASTE, a CLEAR, a LOAD, SOUND or INIT. The screen says what it will undo; press SAVE. Press SAVE again to redo. It is one step: the change before that is gone. It does not undo a save (the slot keeps what was saved), and the looper is apart from it.
  • RANDOM rolls the sound: each SAVE a new roll. KNOB2 is the AMOUNT, 5 .. 100 % (100: NEW SOUND): a little nudges the sound you have (its waves, types and destinations seldom switch, effects that are off stay off), 100 % rolls a new one. KNOB3 picks what may change: ALL, OSC (waves, ratios, modes, levels, the noise, the transients), FILTER, ENV, MOD (the LFOs, the mod envelopes' destinations, the matrix) or FX. Values stay in the ranges that sound (a pitch LFO a vibrato, short attacks more often than long), and the sound is kept audible. The arp, glide, velocity, the split, the VCA and the routing are never touched. Rolls in a row are one UNDO: UNDO brings back the sound before the first. Like it: SAVE it to a slot.
  • TONE is the tone library (below): KNOB1 picks a tone, KNOB2 LOAD or STORE, SAVE does it.

Tones

A tone is one part's sound on its own, as the Roland D-50 kept its tones apart from its patches: the part's oscillator (its waves, modulators, ratios and pitches, envelopes and level), its transient and route, and the part's own filter, amp envelope, LFO, mod envelope 1, noise, velocity and glide. Not in a tone: the other part, the mode, the effects, LFO 2, mod envelope 2, the matrix, the arp and the sequencer. So a tone is something to build a sound from: a bass on A, a pad on B, the effects over both.

The TONE row shows eight tones at a time, each its name and its carrier's wave; the selected one is framed (in the part's colour, two parts playing). There are 160:

  • 96 factory tones, eight of each: BASS, KEYS, PADS, LEADS, BELLS, PLUCKS, ORCH (brass, strings, winds), FX, SYNTH, PERC (toms, cowbell, congas, woodblock ...), VOICE (choirs, formants, robot) and ATMOS (rain, space, deep sea ...). They are fixed.
  • 64 user tones, after them, EMPTY until you store one.

LOAD puts the tone into the part being edited (SELECT picks A or B: see Parts), and leaves the rest of the sound as it was. The screen says where it went: DX BELL INTO B. It is one UNDO step. With one part playing (SINGLE), the tone goes into A, osc 1, and osc 2's level goes down to 0, so you hear the tone as it was made: turn OSC 2's level back up to layer it. A tone sounds the same in A as in B: its modulation of its own oscillator (WAVE1, MOD1 ...) is osc 2's in part B.

STORE keeps the part being edited as a user tone: pick one of the user tones with KNOB1, name it, and press SAVE (over a stored one it asks first: SAVE again). The name is under the line: KNOB3 moves along its nine letters (the one being changed is inverted), KNOB4 turns that letter (A .. Z, 0 .. 9, a space and - . & + / ' #). An empty tone starts as USER and its number, a stored one with its own name (store it again to rename it). The user tones are kept in flash, apart from the patch slots. - The header shows the current slot on every page, with * when there are unsaved changes. - When the FM-1 is switched on, the slot last saved or loaded comes back, and the sequencer that was playing. - Saving takes a moment (the flash is erased): a note playing drops out for about 50 ms. - Patches are kept in two flash sectors written in turn: if the power goes during a save, only that save is lost. - Patches saved by an older Prysm load in a newer one (the new parts start as the default sound has them). From Prysm 0.8 and older: a modulator in MIX or RING gets the pitch of its ratio (x2: +12, x1.5: +7); ratios of 5 and up are further than PIT goes, and come in at +24.

The oscilloscope

Press HOME twice (LIVE, then the scope; or once, with SETUP's HOME on SCOPE) for a live scope of what Prysm plays (before the MASTER knob, so it does not shrink as you turn it down). KNOB2 picks how it draws it:

Style
PRISM (the first) the left channel split like light through a prism: red above, violet below, a white core. The last few traces linger behind it, fading through the colours, and a spectrum glows between the wave and the centre line. A sound that moves (FM, an LFO on the wave) leaves trails
ORBIT the wave across, the same wave a quarter of a cycle later up: a sine draws a circle, FM knots and stars, in colours along the cycle
MONO left in white, right in grey (the chorus and a ping-pong delay show as the two apart)
  • It locks onto the newest note: the trace shows that many of its cycles, starting on a rising zero, so the wave stands still whatever you play. KNOB1 sets how many cycles (1 .. 8; it starts at 2).
  • It zooms by itself (x1 .. x8, shown under the trace), so a single quiet note fills the screen too.
  • HOME again goes back to where you first pressed HOME (with SETUP's HOME on SCOPE: it opens the LIVE screen). ALGORITHM, SAVE or a page button go back too.

The scope costs no sound processing while it is not shown.

USB: audio and MIDI

Connected to a computer, the FM-1 shows up as Prysm FM-1:

  • a 2-in audio input (16-bit stereo, 44.1 or 48 kHz): record Prysm straight into any DAW, no cable needed. It carries exactly what the speaker plays, effects included.
  • a MIDI port: notes in play Prysm (with their velocity, on the SETUP page's IN channel); channel 10 plays the drums; the keys' notes come out on its OUT channel; the MIDI clock can drive the arpeggiator.

Prysm's USB audio is checked on macOS 14, where the audio of many FM-1 firmwares does not appear.

Filming a performance

tools/capture.py makes a video of what you play: the screen exactly as the FM-1 draws it, with its sound, in sync. Connect the FM-1 by USB, then, in the Prysm folder:

.venv/bin/python3 tools/capture.py record mytake

Set the FM-1 up (the page and sound you want to start on, no keys held), press Enter, play, press Enter again. The FM-1 sends what it started from and everything you did (keys, buttons, knob turns, MIDI coming in) to the computer, which plays the take again through Prysm's own code, frame by frame, and writes takes/mytake.mp4 (720 x 720, 30 fps) and takes/mytake/audio.wav. Options: --fps 60, --size 1080, --tail 5 (seconds after the last thing you did, for the release and the reverb), --splash (start with the power-on animation); tools/capture.py render takes/mytake --fps 60 films a take again. It needs ffmpeg (.venv/bin/pip install imageio-ffmpeg, or brew install ffmpeg).

Not in the film: the MASTER knob (it is at full level), and the MIDI clock (a take played on CLOCK = MIDI uses the internal tempo).

The editor

The editor (Chrome or Edge) is Prysm itself, running in the page: the screen, the knobs (drag or scroll), the buttons and the keys (or your computer's keys, A W S E D ... from C4), with the sound. Beside it, a tab for every page with every parameter, the sequencer's 64 steps as a grid, and patch files to save and load. The browser keeps its own 8 slots between visits, and its own user tones. Tone… loads a tone from the library into part A or B (the menu beside it). In the plugin, the user tones are kept with the project. Load patch opens a patch file: the demo sounds show the two parts (a bass under a pad, a piano layered with strings, a bass line and a lead on one pattern ...).

With an FM-1 (Prysm 1.0 or later): connect it by USB and press Connect FM-1 (allow MIDI when the browser asks). The editor takes the FM-1's current sound, and from then on the two stay in step: what you change in the editor the FM-1 plays (its knob's value shows in the corner, as if you had turned it), and what you turn on the FM-1 shows up in the editor. Store on FM-1 saves the sound in one of its slots. Another MIDI keyboard connected to the computer plays the browser's Prysm.

If something goes wrong

  • It will not start / a black screen after an update: hold OCT- while switching the FM-1 on. That starts the rescue updater (no sound, just the update port): install Prysm, or another firmware from the Firmware Hub.
  • A crash: the screen shows where, and the FM-1 restarts. Two crashes in a row at start-up go to the rescue updater by themselves.
  • The installer cannot find the FM-1: use Chrome or Edge, a USB cable that carries data, plugged straight into the computer; close other music apps that may hold the MIDI port.
  • The sound crackles: the CPU is over its limit; lower the effects or the number of notes.

Sound ideas

  • Electric piano: OSC 1 carrier sine, mod 1 sine at x1 (or x14 for the tine), FM, a quick mod 1 attack and a medium release; AMP with a long decay and low sustain; chorus.
  • Evolving pad: carrier on VOCAL, LFO TRI slow into WAVE1, mod envelope slow attack into LFOAMT; reverb with a big size and some shimmer.
  • Rhythmic bleeps: LFO S&H into MPITCH with some FM; mod envelope LOOP ON into LFORATE; delay with ping and a little shift.
  • Bass: carrier saw, osc 2 a square an octave down (MIX: -12 st), filter low with a fast envelope and some drive.
  • Bells: carrier sine, mod 1 sine at x3 (or x1.5, x7), FM with a long release; delay with shift +12 and the tone down.
